# Product Principles v0.1

These principles are the current product constitution for LearnWard. Features that conflict with them should be rejected or explicitly revisited at the product-definition level.

## 1. Reward growth, not obedience

Recognize:
- improvement,
- mastery,
- meaningful effort,
- recovery from mistakes,
- consistency,
- self-chosen goals.

Do not center the product on “obey parent → receive reward.”

## 2. Achievement and improvement both matter

High performers should not be penalized for already being strong, while improving learners should not lose simply because their absolute score is lower.

The system therefore needs both:
- achievement recognition,
- growth recognition.

## 3. Effort can create progression; reward currency must remain bounded

Study XP may be broadly earned and should represent cumulative learning activity.

Reward Points must be more tightly controlled to avoid farming and unsustainable family reward costs.

## 4. Never take away earned progression for poor performance

Do not remove:
- XP,
- previously earned RP,
- rewards,
- levels,
- historical achievements

because of a low score or missed day.

Failure should create recovery opportunities rather than punitive debt.

## 5. Child autonomy is a first-class requirement

Where practical, children should be able to choose:
- personal goals,
- reward categories,
- whether to spin or save,
- challenges,
- which weakness to work on next.

## 6. Parents define boundaries, not every interaction

Parents should control:
- reward inventory,
- reward costs,
- budgets,
- verification policy,
- safety constraints.

They should not need to manually micromanage every low-value learning event.

## 7. No monetized randomness

Never support:
- purchasing spins,
- purchasing RP for spins,
- ads-for-spins,
- paid rerolls,
- cash-out,
- real-money wagering,
- paid probability boosts.

The wheel is a family celebration mechanic, not a monetization engine.

## 8. No losing spins

A spin should not produce:
- “better luck next time,”
- zero reward,
- loss of RP,
- near-miss manipulation.

Every valid spin produces a parent-approved outcome.

## 9. Do not optimize for addiction metrics

Primary product success is not:
- time in app,
- number of spins,
- number of notifications opened,
- maximum DAU at any cost.

Engagement is useful only when it corresponds to real learning value.

## 10. No sibling academic leaderboard by default

Avoid direct ranking of siblings by grades or XP.

Prefer:
- personal bests,
- individual mastery,
- cooperative family quests.

## 11. Progress must be explainable

If LearnWard awards RP, XP, mastery, or a growth label, parent and child should be able to understand why.

Avoid opaque scoring models in early versions.

## 12. Never make low scores feel like product failure

A low result should lead naturally to:
- diagnosis,
- review,
- recovery quest,
- achievable next goal.

## 13. Reward economy must be sustainable for the family

The application must not create obligations parents cannot reasonably fulfill.

Eventually support:
- reward stock,
- cooldowns,
- availability windows,
- monthly reward budgets.

## 14. External learning is a core assumption

LearnWard should not require the child to learn inside LearnWard for the system to work.

School tests, tutors, paper worksheets, books, and third-party platforms are legitimate learning sources.

## 15. Mastery should gradually matter more than rewards

The product should progressively shift attention toward:
- skill maps,
- personal records,
- challenging goals,
- recovery,
- self-directed improvement.

The objective is not to eliminate rewards abruptly, but to reduce their psychological centrality over time.
