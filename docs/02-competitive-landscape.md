# Competitive Landscape v0.1

## Executive summary

The original concept — **learning/tasks → points → wheel → parent-defined reward** — is not unique. Multiple products already implement substantial parts of this loop.

The opportunity for LearnWard is therefore not to build a better wheel. The stronger white space is:

> **External academic results → verified progress → growth-aware scoring → mastery → family motivation economy.**

## Market categories

### A. Learning + rewards
Products in this group combine academic activity with a reward system.

Examples:
- Wonder
- Reward Study
- ClassDojo (partially, especially home rewards and learning ecosystem)

### B. Real-world tasks + game/reward loop
Products in this group are strong at verification, chores, quests, pets, wheels, and family administration, but weak at academic intelligence.

Examples:
- ParentPal
- Joon
- Doozy
- MeowTasks
- StarBloom
- S'moresUp

## Competitor notes

### Wonder
Closest to the original LearnWard concept.

Strengths:
- Grade 3–8 focus,
- adaptive academic content,
- internal knowledge of effort and performance,
- currency and parent-configured reward wheel.

Strategic weakness relative to LearnWard's target positioning:
- learning largely happens inside Wonder,
- less naturally positioned as an aggregation layer across school, tutoring, paper tests, books, and third-party tools.

### Reward Study
Important because it connects learning activities with a family credit/reward economy.

Strengths:
- parent approval,
- reward store,
- levels/badges/streaks,
- learning modules and worksheets,
- some support for paper-based work.

Potential gap:
- no clear evidence of a unified longitudinal growth model that rewards improvement, difficulty, personal baseline, recovery, and mastery together.

### ParentPal
Important local reference for Vietnam.

Strengths:
- real-world tasks,
- photo evidence,
- AI-assisted verification,
- parent approval,
- points/wallet/rewards,
- pet/progression mechanics.

Strategic distinction:
- core positioning is closer to independence, chores, and financial habits than academic growth intelligence.

### ClassDojo
Not the closest functional competitor, but potentially the strongest distribution threat.

Strengths:
- school/teacher/parent ecosystem,
- established family usage,
- home points and rewards,
- broad education presence.

Gap relevant to LearnWard:
- no clear unified external academic Growth Engine connecting test performance, improvement, mastery, and family rewards.

### Joon
Strong reference for game-based motivation.

Strengths:
- real-world quests affect virtual pet progression,
- emotional attachment and game progression,
- strong child-facing design.

Risk to learn from:
- novelty decay. A virtual world can create early excitement without guaranteeing sustained learning motivation.

### Doozy
Strong implementation reference for random rewards and verification.

Useful patterns:
- child completes a real-world task,
- evidence can be submitted,
- parent approves,
- reward wheel is separated from monetization.

LearnWard should copy the philosophy of low-stakes, parent-bounded randomness — not the chore-centric product.

### MeowTasks / StarBloom
These demonstrate that the combination of:
- XP,
- tickets,
- wheel,
- pets,
- streaks,
- parent-defined prizes

is not a defensible moat.

### S'moresUp
Useful primarily as a product-scope warning.

A family app can accumulate chores, allowance, calendars, communication, reports, penalties, automation, and many adjacent functions. LearnWard should resist this expansion until the core learning loop is proven.

## Strategic conclusion

Do not compete on:
- more animations,
- more badges,
- more spins,
- more pets,
- more generic family-management features.

Compete on:

```text
Academic Intelligence
        +
External Learning Capture
        +
Motivation System
```

## Proposed moat ladder

```text
1. Growth Engine
        ↓
2. Longitudinal learning history
        ↓
3. Personal baseline model
        ↓
4. Difficulty/mastery calibration
        ↓
5. Cross-source learning graph
        ↓
6. Personalized next-challenge prediction
```

The wheel is a feature. The learner model is the potential moat.
